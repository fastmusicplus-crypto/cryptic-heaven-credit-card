# Vision and scope

## Working vision

Explore a payment-card product that may let eligible customers access spending power supported by crypto collateral, with understandable limits, transparent costs, reliable transaction handling, and clear customer protections.

This is a hypothesis to validate—not a promise that the product will be offered, approved, or available in any particular market.

## Customer problem to investigate

Potential customers may hold digital assets but want familiar card-based purchasing. Discovery should establish whether they want:

- To borrow against assets without selling them.
- Predictable spending limits and repayment terms.
- Clear explanations of collateral risk and what happens during price movements.
- Dependable card acceptance, transaction visibility, and responsive support.

Do not assume that these needs imply a credit product. Prepaid, debit, or partner-issued alternatives may be more feasible or appropriate.

## Candidate outcomes

The product should only advance if research shows that it can provide:

- A clear, understandable relationship between collateral, available spending, debt, and repayment.
- Consistent authorization and settlement outcomes, including declines and reversals.
- Timely notices and fair processes for repayment difficulties and collateral shortfalls.
- Secure handling of account and payment information.
- Transparent fees and meaningful support and dispute-resolution paths.

## Initial scope for discovery

Explore—but do not pre-commit to—the following:

- One clearly defined customer segment and a limited set of target markets.
- A narrow, partner-supported asset and payment configuration.
- A documented credit or non-credit product model.
- The full lifecycle: eligibility, funding, authorization, clearing, repayment, support, and account closure.
- Operational ownership, incident response, and customer communications.

## Out of scope until specifically validated

- Announcing availability, benefits, rates, or approvals to customers.
- Selecting a chain or token standard solely because the original concept mentioned smart contracts.
- Issuing cards, extending credit, holding customer assets, or processing live transactions.
- Treating an internal prototype, test token, or partner conversation as production authorization.
- Expanding to multiple jurisdictions or collateral types before the first operating model is understood.

## Principles

1. **Customer clarity:** Explain balances, borrowing, fees, and collateral consequences in plain language.
2. **Partner and legal validation:** Establish who is responsible for each regulated and operational activity.
3. **Minimize custody and sensitive data:** Collect and retain only what the approved operating model requires.
4. **Fail safely:** Prefer a clear decline or pause over an uncertain authorization, stale price, or incomplete settlement.
5. **No unsupported promises:** Do not imply guaranteed card acceptance, stable collateral value, or assured repayment outcomes.
6. **Evidence before scale:** Expand only after measured operational and risk outcomes support doing so.
